Work quietly. Not every effort needs an audience.

Yes and no.
Sometimes, you really do need to lock in.
You don't need to announce every plan, document every step, or turn your process into content. Some things need room to grow without the pressure of people watching.
But sometimes, working quietly can become an excuse to work blindly.
You can spend six months "building in silence" only to discover that nobody needs what you've been building.
Sometimes, you need to show your work.
Get feedback. Ask questions. Let people poke holes in the idea. Allow someone who knows better to tell you, "Omo, this thing no make sense." or "This would have been nice in 1990, but in big 2026? there are better ways to go about this"😂
There's a difference between seeking validation and seeking perspective.
You don't need everybody's opinion.
If you're building something, find people who understand the problem you're trying to solve.
If you're learning, find people who know more than you.
If you're creating, find people who are actually your audience.
Because feedback from the wrong people can make you abandon a good idea, while feedback from the right ones can expose what you couldn't see yourself.
So yes, lock in.
Just don't lock yourself away.
Not everything needs an audience.
But some things need the right one.
