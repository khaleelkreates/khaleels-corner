Have you ever wondered why we sometimes find it so difficult to let go of things, even when we know they've stopped adding value to our lives?
A relationship, a business idea, a career path, an expectation, or sometimes the an old t-shirt we've held onto for years 😂. Sometimes, we keep holding on simply because we've invested so much in them.
Interestingly, accounting has a concept that offers an interesting way to look at this: Asset Impairment.
In simple terms, when an asset's carrying value in the books is higher than the amount expected to be recovered from it, the difference is recognised as an impairment loss and its carrying value is adjusted.
And I think there's a lesson in that for everyday life.
The fact that something meant so much to you in the past doesn't necessarily mean it still deserves the same place in your present.
There's also something called the sunk cost fallacy. It's when we keep investing in something simply because we've already invested so much, even when continuing no longer makes sense.
It's a bit like having a wardrobe full of clothes but constantly saying, "I have nothing to wear." Maybe it's not that you lack clothes. You've simply held on to so many old ones that you can barely make sense of what you have anymore. 😂
Think about it. You wouldn't keep carrying an asset at an unrealistic value simply because you paid more for it years ago. So why should your relationships, expectations or personal decisions be any different?
You need to acknowledge what has changed, accept what cannot be recovered, adjust your expectations and move forward.
It might hurt to write it down, but carrying an unrealistic value doesn't make something more valuable. It only distorts your balance sheet.
Not everything you lose is a loss. Sometimes, it's just time to write it down.
